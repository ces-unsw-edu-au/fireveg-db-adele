# Trait Logic — Interpretation Record

This file documents how raw data from each paper was mapped to fireveg database values, and flags cases where the mapping required interpretation, inference, or expert judgement. It is intended for expert review before records are approved for database import.

**Status key in mapping files:** `approved` = ready for R script; `skip` = excluded from processing as the logic or evidence wasn't strong enough (but mapping still be documented here for reference).

### Flags for expert review
Currently, this is the trait that has the highest number of records in the database, and most have been mapped to All or None.  

Our logic also uses All or None most of the time, often from repsrouter / reseeder status - but it's obviously imperfect.  For example, a species marked as an obligate seeder may have resprout potential, but would be marked as 'None' for resprouting. 

Ideas for improving mapping of this trait might be:
- determining whether a Fire Category score (from Gill & Bradstock, 1992), and therefore regenerative organ might be more associated with 'mostly' or 'sometimes' resprouting e.g. root suckers vs. tubers vs epicormic, rather than having to correct this on a species level. 
- More detailed split added at a family level? 

### Notes 
- **Clarke Knox 2002: uncertain entries.** Several species have `*` (inferred from morphology), `Variable`, or `Obligate seeder (75%)` as raw values. These are excluded from records.

- **Morrison 1995: Ft category.** The `Ft` (fire tolerant) classification does not distinguish between resprouting mechanisms. Without knowing what proportion of individuals resprout, a surv1 value cannot be assigned. 

- **Williams Ashton 1988: paper does not study fire response.** The 1939 wildfire is referenced as historical context only. Morphological trait presence (dormant buds, root stock) is used as a proxy for resprouting capacity — this is an inference, not a direct observation.

### Flags for expert review

- **Auld 1991 / Purdie 1977 / Purdie Slatyer 1976: family-level inference → PY.** Physical dormancy is assigned to all Fabaceae on the basis of the hard-seed-coat trait that is common in the family. This is biologically defensible but is an inference, not a species-level measurement in these papers. Confirm this is acceptable for import. It is based on Auld 1991 explicitly stating that all Fabaceae species studies are PY and this has been extrapolated to Purdie and Purdie Slatyer.

### Notes

- **repr3 vs repr3a routing.** Where a paper reports a single time-to-first-flowering measure for all species, the species' regeneration mode decides which trait it becomes: seed-only regenerators → repr3, resprouters → repr3a. `raw_value` on these records carries both the flowering value and the regeneration-mode value that routed it, so the decision is auditable from the record alone.

Same numerical conventions as repr3.

|---|---|
| **Total** | **664** (numerical — best/lower/upper) |

### Mappings by paper

| Paper | Source | Mapping logic | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Van Moezel Loneragan Bell 1987 | Appendix Juvenile Period column, `Sprout` and `Sprout & Seed` species only | Same as repr3 but for resprouter species | approved |
| Wark 1997 | Table 5 codes F1/F2/F3, ORR and FRR species only | Same as repr3 | approved |
| Purdie Slatyer 1976 | Text | Flowering in first growing season noted but not expressed numerically | skip |

### Flags for expert review

- **Wark 1997: FRR species assigned to repr3a is an inference, not stated by the source.** Table 5's ORR section is headed "Obligate regrowth regenerators (by regrowth only)", so ORR → repr3a is stated — those species have no post-fire seedling cohort and a flowering individual must be a resprout. FRR (facultative regrowth regenerator) species regenerate both by regrowth *and* from seed, and the paper does not say which cohort was observed flowering. They are assigned to repr3a because only the resprout cohort can flower on the observed timescale: the FRR group includes all eucalypts, e.g. *Eucalyptus obliqua* coded `St1, L1 S1 F2` — germinated from seed in year 1, first flowered in year 2. A eucalypt seedling does not flower at 2 years, so the observation is the resprouting cohort. Routing FRR to repr3 instead would assert "flowers from seed at 2 years" for a eucalypt. 56 records depend on this inference. The alternative is to exclude FRR from both traits.

- **Van Moezel Loneragan Bell 1987: combined-class species routed on first-listed mode.** Appendix 1 uses both `Sprout & Seed` and `Seed & Sprout`, and the header describes the column as the "most preferred fire response mode" — so the first-listed mode is read as dominant and decides the trait. 9 records (of 15 combined-class species) depend on this reading. Single-mode species (`Seed` → repr3, `Sprout` → repr3a) are unaffected; the Discussion explicitly treats sprouting species' juvenile period as a sprouter measure.
