## germ8 — Seed dormancy type

**Status:** approved

**Source column:** Results — Seed germination section; Fig. 1; Discussion

**Notes:** The paper reports that *Prostanthera askania* seed has high levels of dormancy: only 10–15% of seeds germinated in the absence of smoke. Smoke produced a partial dormancy break. The paper also notes that germination consistently occurred via a seed plug, suggesting a physical dormancy mechanism may exist (Lamiaceae species generally not regarded as having physical dormancy per Baskin and Baskin 1998). The paper discusses the dormancy as potentially physiological (after-ripening) and partially broken by smoke (a chemical cue). The combined evidence points to Physiological dormancy partially broken by smoke, with possible Physical component (seed plug). This is a single-species study.

## germ1 — Seedbank type

**Status:** approved

**Source column:** Discussion (p. 301); phenology results

**Notes:** The paper provides evidence for a soil seedbank in *Prostanthera askania*: a fire in October 2002 resulted in germination across an entire site despite the population being burnt before flowering onset. Assuming seed shed phenology is consistent across years (fruits shed October–February, fire in October), germination must have come from seed dispersed at least 4 months prior, implying a soil seedbank persisting at least 4 months. The paper explicitly states "This provides evidence of the existence of a soil seedbank." Seed dormancy is consistent with a persistent seedbank. However, duration is uncertain — the evidence only confirms persistence for months, not years. Classification as Soil-persistent vs Transient cannot be definitively resolved from this paper alone.

## rect2 — Establishment pattern

**Status:** skip

**Source column:** Discussion (p. 301); Introduction

**Notes:** The paper notes that the *P. askania* population burnt in October 2002 regenerated by seedling establishment (Department of Environment and Conservation 2004), and that germination of P. askania seed is maximised by smoke without heat (low-intensity fire cue). The paper explicitly states that low-intensity fire may be optimal for germination of this species. This is consistent with Post-fire enhanced recruitment, but the paper does not directly compare post-fire vs continuous seedling establishment rates per se. Only one species studied.

## repr3 — Age at first flower production from seed

**Status:** skip

**Source column:** Introduction; Methods (study species description)

**Notes:** The paper notes that other *Prostanthera* species have features consistent with pioneer strategy "including the production of flowers within a few years of seedling establishment" (Tierney and Gross 2001), and that the P. askania population burnt in October 2002 had "some limited flowering of these seedlings" observed by time of writing (~2005/2006, i.e. 3–4 years post-fire). This implies first flowering within approximately 3–4 years from seed. However, this is described as "limited" and is not a precise age measurement. Single species only.
